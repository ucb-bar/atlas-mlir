# Verification of RTL timing facts

Four layers check the facts against the compiler and against simulated hardware. None qualifies operations or configurations outside the extracted scope.

## Extractor tests

`test/test_rtl_extract_*.py` cover the interpreter, the coupled recipe and spec validation on synthetic circuits, and (with `ATLAS_HW_IR` and `ATLAS_HW_EXPORTER` set) reproduce every value in the [results table](README.md#results). Mutations such as an extra register stage or a payload-dependent control path must move or reject the result. `test/test_rtl_extract_pairfacts.py` covers pair facts: records join their pair and sequence files, the live capacity is what the accepted runs show, and unchecked, unresolved, mismatched or duplicate inputs fail; with `ATLAS_OP_TIMING` it builds the VPU document (841 records, 402 at gap 1, capacity 2). It also covers the MXU window (a command without `next_issue_age` is live through its last event age), accumulator and weight-slot relations, duplicate records across files and an engine whose blocks would take different windows. `test_rtl_extract_pairs.py` checks that `pairs/core_mxu_same.yaml` and `pairs/core_mxu_shared.yaml` declare every same-MXU command pair, accumulator and weight-slot relation and MREG overlap, with instruction words that encode them, and with hardware inputs reproduces every stable gap with the gap below rejected for its recorded reason; `test_rtl_extract_sequences.py` does the same for the 172 chain records of `sequences/core_mxu.yaml`. The selected document combines the VPU pairs and chains with both MXU pair files (1,573 records; capacity two on the VPU and on each MXU).

## Compiler cross-check

`test/test_rtl_extract_compiler.py` compares each extracted record with the compiler's built-in footprints (`lib/AtlasTiming.cpp`, via `test/rtl-timing-facts-probe.cpp`) and fails on any difference. `test/rtl-timing-facts-map.yaml` maps records to compiler mnemonics and states the conventions; every record is compared or listed as not modeled. This detects drift between the compiler's constants and the RTL. Commands are in the [extractor reference](../../tools/rtl_extract/README.md#compiler-cross-check).

`test/test_rtl_gap_coverage.py` checks pair sweeps the other way round: for every `rtl_extract.op_pairs.v1` record (`ATLAS_PAIR_SWEEPS`, default every `pairs-*.json` beside `ATLAS_OP_TIMING`; system pairs are mapped by decoding their VLS, transpose, VPU, MXU and DMA instruction words and requiring the compiler to encode the same word, with the VLS base and DMA operands that arrive on cuts materialized in registers), the smallest gap `--verify-atlas-timing` accepts under the selected provider must be at least the record's `stable_gap`. A DMA transfer's beats are not compiler facts, so for a pair with a DMA launch a gap whose only failure is a moved DMA event (the transfer queued, or its VMEM accesses waiting for the LSU) counts as accepted, and the bound is the gap after the last other failure; two launches never keep isolated timing (the second is queued) and bind launch order only. A record names only the earliest mismatching event group, so `test_rtl_extract_dma.py` checks the other operation's events directly. The MXU and DMA system sweeps (`pairs-core_mxu.json`, `pairs-core_mxu_cross.json`, `pairs-core_mxu_units.json`, `pairs-core_dma.json`, `pairs-core_dma_mxu.json`) are passed through `ATLAS_PAIR_SWEEPS` with the default set. It prints the slack, and, for information, the built-in rules' gaps, none of which falls below the bound. Over the default set plus the MXU and DMA system sweeps (1,326 records), every supported record meets its bound (768 core, 78 DMA, 4 VLSU and 4 VPU records and one MXU1 engine record with zero slack; the MXU system records at zero slack are 51 of `core_mxu`, 150 of `core_mxu_cross`, all 104 of `core_mxu_units` and all 56 of `core_dma_mxu`, while same-MXU pairs keep the busy rule's slack); the one unsupported record is the VSTORE onto a pending transfer's own lines, which the provider rejects. This check found the VSTORE source release: a VPU, XLU or MXU pop writing a VSTORE's source register fires the MREG bank tracker's WAR assertion one cycle after the read release, so the provider holds such writers to the VSTORE's `first_free_age`. With `ATLAS_PAIR_FACTS` the pair facts are selected too: every supported record still meets its bound, and every record of `pairs-core_vpu.json`, the 402 overlapping pairs included, now has zero slack (counted before the MXU and DMA additions above: 867 core, 22 DMA, 4 VLSU, 5 VPU and one MXU1 record with zero slack). The system sweeps are split between `out/` and `out/system/`, so coverage lists them all in `ATLAS_PAIR_SWEEPS`, with the same-MXU sweeps `pairs-core_mxu_same.json` (196 records) and `pairs-core_mxu_shared.json` (536): of the 2,058 records, 2,057 are supported both ways and none falls below its bound. Without pair facts the busy rule keeps every same-MXU gap at or above its bound (6 disjoint and 126 shared-operand records at zero slack, 987 records in all); with them 2,000 records have zero slack, among them 179 disjoint and 508 shared-operand same-MXU records, and the rest of the same-MXU slack is the accumulator access rule (a push followed on one accumulator by a pop or accumulating matmul at 2, not 1), the weight-slot retention (a weight push followed by a matmul on its slot at 33, a matmul followed by a weight push on its slot at 94 on MXU0 and 34 on MXU1); where shared operands bind, the MREG release and port rules meet the RTL gap exactly.

## Selected-mode tests

`test/test_rtl_timing.py` and, for MXU, `test_rtl_mxu_timing.py` run scheduling, delay insertion and `--verify-atlas-timing` with the facts selected, and check that wrong digests, a missing or contradicted provider, unsupported domains, early or missing terminals and unsafe streams are rejected, and that admission numbers come from the facts rather than constants. `test_blocks_are_checked_under_each_concrete_entry_context` checks entry contexts: a decided branch reaches only its arm, an undecided diamond is checked under each arm's base and rejected with the context named when one is misaligned, a strided loop is accepted per iteration and rejected in the iteration whose tile leaves VMEM, a join whose contexts differ in VMEM bank gets the worst context's delay, and past the 4096-entry bound an invariant tile still times while a moving one is rejected with a note naming the bound. Final verification rechecks timing from the actual instruction stream, also in `atlas-emit` and the LLVM handoff, so rewrites after scheduling cannot silently invalidate it. `test/test_rtl_virtual_timing.py` lowers virtual programs untimed and times them under the provider: serial-DMA programs with VPU, XLU, MXU and control flow pass both consumers, emission and the LLVM handoff; a selection made before lowering or kept through a reorder-only schedule still names the provider; two-launch lowerings and manual multichannel streams compile where the queued-transfer rules hold, as do the virtual schedules of `virtual_sched_overlap`, `virtual_dma_mxu` and `virtual_dma_mxu_bf16`, which put MXU work beside a pending transfer (kept under delay insertion), while VPU operands sharing a physical bank are rejected by name; `--schedule-atlas-virtual` costs from the facts when they are selected; and both FP8 two-layer MLP programs (with and without bias) compile with their pack relayout loop checked under each of its 32 iterations, which the export lists as entry contexts. `test/timing-provider-verification.cpp` (CTest `atlas-timing-provider`) covers the registry and the common verifier's policy isolation, including the physical scheduler's use of a non-model provider.

`test/test_rtl_pair_facts.py` checks the pair facts selection: recorded with its digest, reported by the export, rechecked on every load, and rejected for a wrong digest, a missing digest, another hardware IR, other facts, an unchecked record, an unknown block or a malformed stable gap or capacity. `test/test_rtl_vpu_slots.py` (with `ATLAS_PAIR_FACTS`) checks VPU admission under pair facts: representative overlapping and serial pairs are accepted at the fact's stable gap and rejected one cycle earlier (or, at gap 1, by the default selection), both consumers place them there, operands sharing a physical bank, a missing fact or a fact without a known relation fall back to `next_issue_age`, a third command waits for a free slot, the XLU waits for every live VPU command, all eighteen chain results of `sequences/core_vpu.yaml` are reproduced as the smallest accepted uniform spacing, and without pair facts the timing is unchanged.

`test/test_rtl_mxu_pipeline.py` (with `ATLAS_PAIR_FACTS`) checks same-MXU admission: every one of the 196 disjoint-operand facts is accepted at the larger of its stable gap and the accumulator and weight access distance and rejected one cycle earlier; every one of the 536 shared-operand facts is accepted at the first gap the compiler admits, never below its stable gap, and where both commands only read the shared registers exactly at that larger gap; representative pairs issue inside the first command's busy window under both consumers; a shared-bank pop takes its overlap's fact and falls back to the busy end without it; a missing record, an unknown relation key or value, a relation leaving the accumulator unstated and a sweep that stops short of the busy end all fall back to the busy rule; a third matmul waits for the facts' capacity of two (a capacity of three would admit it); every one of the 172 chain records of `sequences/core_mxu.yaml` is rejected by the compiler at every uniform spacing below its stable gap, 109 are reproduced exactly and the rest are held back by the capacity or an access rule named at the stable gap; the export reports the pipelined units; and MXU programs time identically with no pair facts and with pair facts that carry no MXU records.

`test/test_rtl_overlap.py` checks each admission rule at its boundary for every first/second combination of vload, vstore, VPU, XLU and MXU: accepted at the facts-derived gap and rejected one cycle earlier with the busy unit and first permitted cycle named, with both consumers issuing at that boundary; an MXU command overlaps every other engine and the other MXU at gap 1, and a VPU, XLU or MXU pop writing a VSTORE's source waits for the VSTORE's `first_free_age` while a VLOAD follows the read release. `test_rtl_mxu_timing.py` accepts every MXU command against the other MXU, VPU, XLU and VLS at gap 1 in both orders, puts shared-register (RAW, WAR) and shared-bank boundaries at their facts-derived ages and rejects them one cycle earlier, keeps same-MXU commands at the busy rule, and issues MXU work beside a pending transfer under both consumers.

## Simulation

**Compiler-scheduled programs on Verilator.** `tools/replay-ee290-compute.py --facts F.json [--pairs P.json] --bin <dir with atlas-opt, atlas-emit> --work DIR (--model VAtlasCore | --rtl <dir of .sv/.v>)` runs every case in `tools/ee290_replay_cases/` (or `--cases a,b`) on AtlasCore with the facts selected and `dma=wait`, and prints `PASS <case>` or exits nonzero. A case is data: instructions, initial and expected VMEM/DRAM, and expected command counts; a new file in that package is discovered automatically. The model must be built with the current `test/ee290-compute-replay.cpp`, which loads a case image and checks every initialized byte. The validator follows the PC through branches with the one delay slot, requires every engine idle and no DMA pending at each block entry, tracks one live command per engine, and attributes every port event to its engine. `tools/check-ee290-compute-export.py` binds each dynamic block instance to the block-relative offsets of `atlas-emit --rtl-timing-json`, and each command's accesses (address and age) and release to its own footprint; holds on other engines may outlast the command but not end before it. Hand-scheduled cases (`final=True`) bind footprints to a delay-inserted reference instead of issue offsets. DMA completion is anchored to the matching wait, never to a latency. The cases cover transpose, BF16 multiply, DMA→transpose, a loop, a DMA loop, both arms of an if/else, and two overlap streams (vload with vstore on distinct banks one cycle apart; a VPU multiply with a vload one cycle later), where the hardware kept each command's isolated timing. Relaxation cases run the compiler's own overlapping schedules with both consumers and require the overlap to occur: vmul→vmul at 65 (the second issues on the edge of the first's last write, admitted by the RTL's issue-busy bit), vload/vstore on distinct banks at 1 and on one bank at exactly 30 and 34, a VPU under a vload at 1, and a pipelined load-transpose-store. The validator follows VectorFSM's two slots: beside live VPU work a VPU command needs its issue-busy bit clear and its port pairs free (held by no command, or by one whose last write fires on that edge); a two-port command takes both pairs, a single-port command the first free one, events are attributed per port to the oldest command still expecting them and recorded on the command's own port, and a command with company releases when its events are done and no write marker names its destination. Cases marked `pairs` are scheduled with `--pairs` pair facts selected (skipped without). The validator keeps every live command of an MXU, attributes each port, accumulator, weight and compute event to the oldest live command still expecting one on that port and resource, and releases a command with company once its events are done and no live marker names an operand only it uses; the binder still holds each command to its isolated footprint. `mxu_pipeline.py` runs the MXU tile and accumulate cases with pair facts (the pop 64 cycles into its matmul on MXU0, 4 on MXU1), two tiles on one unit (weight push one cycle into the other slot's matmul, matmuls 32 apart on MXU0, pops 32 apart), a K=2 accumulation (the second weight push one cycle into the first matmul) and #15's virtual_dma_mxu and FP8 two-layer MLPs (shared-operand facts), each under both consumers with exact results and a check that two commands of one unit were live together; `test/test_ee290_compute_replay.py` mutates them (a dropped accumulator write or a moved pop row during the overlap, a shifted event or a borrowed release is rejected). `vpu_slots.py` runs exp beside relu (one cycle apart under delay insertion, five scheduled), a two-port add followed by relu, mov and an immediate fill where the third command takes the first slot to free up on the edge of its last write (both consumers), and the replay's exp, exp2, square, cube and mov stream overlapped by delay insertion, all with exact lane data. Two cases run one FP8 tile through each MXU (weight push, overwrite matmul, BF16 pop; MXU1 on slot and accumulator 1): the validator checks weights and activations against their MREG rows and pops against the recorded accumulator, MXU busy is derived from the sequencer's port engines and in-flight tracker, and expected results come from an exact fixed-point reference. Accumulator writes landed at ages 63–94 (MXU0) and 3–34 (MXU1), as extracted. Seven MXU overlap cases per consumer (`mxu_overlap.py`) run that tile on each MXU with a DMA load or store launched during the matmul, beside an FP8 unpack of the activation tile, a transpose of the weight tile, VSTOREs and a VLOAD, or on both MXUs at once; every case's check requires the MXU commands to have been live together with the transfer, with the VPU, XLU, VLOAD and VSTORE commands, or with the other MXU. #15's `virtual_dma_mxu` program, virtually scheduled and lowered untimed, then delay-inserted from the facts, pushes its weights while the activation load is pending and produces an exact FP8 readout; its scale register is set by SELI. An MXU footprint must carry exactly one own-unit hold, on its own MXU, ending at the observed release. The replay decodes every VPU operation (binary, unary, row and column reductions, FP8 pack/unpack with the scale register read at issue, immediate loads) and checks written rows against exact lane references where one exists (relu, mov, max/min, immediate loads and pack/unpack bit for bit; add/sub, square/cube and sums only where exact; exp on special values and exp2 on integers); other transcendentals are rejected. MXU accumulator pushes (FP8 dequantized, BF16 pairs), accumulating matmuls (checked against the accumulator) and FP8 pops (scale register read at issue) run on both MXUs, and SELD binds to its VMEM read at age 1 and scale write at age 3. Several DMA commands may be pending on distinct channels: requests follow launch order, responses their TileLink source, and each completion precedes its own channel's wait; hand-scheduled streams bind to a reference in which each wait follows its launch. All new operations matched their facts ages. One case is a virtual program (`tools/ee290_replay_cases/virtual.py`: BF16 input → ReLU → output) that the driver verifies, schedules, allocates and lowers untimed before selecting the facts; its output bytes, input and guard bytes, VMEM staging windows and completion are checked like every other case, and `test/test_ee290_compute_replay.py` checks that its lowering is untimed, its final stream is timed by `rtl-op-timing-v1`, and its LLVM handoff reproduces the emitted words. `tools/ee290_replay_cases/fp8_mlp.py` runs the FP8 two-layer MLP virtual programs, the plain one lowered with and without the virtual scheduler and the one with BF16 biases (a VPU add after each MXU): two MXU tiles, ReLU, FP8 pack (SELI writes the scale register as it fires) and the 32-iteration LW/SW relayout loop whose addresses move every iteration. The validator decodes LW (VMEM read, response, register write), SW (masked word write, data checked against the register) and SELI; each dynamic block instance records its known entry registers, and the binder takes the footprints of the entry-context group they match, so every LW/SW binds its VMEM line at age 1 and an LW its register write at age 3 under its own iteration's addresses; the output, intermediate tiles and guard bytes match exact references. 80 runs pass (111 with `--pairs`). A vload and vstore on the same VMEM bank one cycle apart trips the RTL assertion "VLOAD and VSTORE target same VMEM bank". `test/test_ee290_compute_replay.py` runs it when `ATLAS_EE290_COMPUTE_MODEL`, `ATLAS_OOT_BIN_DIR` and `ATLAS_OP_TIMING` are set, and mutates saved traces to check rejections.

**Issue #11 VCS witness.** `tools/run-ee290-vls-witness.py --program test/examples/ee290_vls_copy.mlir --facts F --atlas-opt PATH --atlas-emit PATH --simulator simv --host-cc <riscv gcc> --output DIR [--schedule delay|schedule]` selects the facts, schedules or inserts delays, verifies, emits, builds the host program and runs `simv +loadmem`; it exits 0 on pass, 1 on a failed or license-unavailable run and 2 on a tool error. It checks outputs and guard memory, not event timing. Earlier Verilator and VCS waveform captures of the VLS copy kernel matched the compiler's predicted events cycle for cycle.

**DMA.** Simulation cannot bound external completion, so the `dma=wait` policy rests on structure: completion only at the matching wait, and verification that every launched transfer is waited before reuse, publication or halt. The queued-transfer rules rest on the assertion-checked AtlasCore pair sweeps of `targets/atlas/pairs/core_dma.yaml` (`test/test_rtl_extract_dma.py`) and on Verilator streams of concurrent transfers (`tools/ee290_replay_cases/dma_pair.py`, `dma_concurrency.py`): launches on other channels queue with requests in launch order, channel busy is per channel and clears when the slot retires, VLS, VPU, XLU and MXU keep their isolated events while a transfer is pending (`pairs/core_dma_mxu.yaml` for every MXU command on both units, the transfer not delayed), and a same-bank VLS only delays the transfer's VMEM accesses. The RTL checks neither DMA/VLS address overlap nor same-channel reuse, so disjoint windows, the per-channel limit and the eight-slot rule stay compiler rules.
